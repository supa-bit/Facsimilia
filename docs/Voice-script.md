## The opening (the bard)

- **opening_01_gobekli** (bard): [slowly] Listen. Before the first field was sown, before the first village, the hunters of the hills came together. On a ridge above the plain of Harran they raised great pillars of stone, carved with foxes, boars and vultures, and set them in rings. It was a place of gathering more than a home: they came to build, to feast, and to remember. This is Göbekli Tepe, more than eleven thousand years ago, the oldest place we know where many hands worked as one.
- **opening_02_grain** (bard): In those same hills grew wild wheat. Year after year the people gathered it, kept the best seed, and sowed it again, until the grass of the hills became einkorn, and the hunters became farmers. Sheep and goats followed them home. At Jericho, by a spring in the valley of the Jordan, men raised a wall and a tower of stone, ten thousand years ago. [pause] Bread had made them stay.
- **opening_03_catal** (bard): On the plain of Konya the houses of Çatalhöyük stood wall against wall, a town with no streets. Its people walked across the rooftops and climbed down ladders into their homes. They painted bulls and leopards on the plaster, set horns into the walls, and buried their dead beneath the floors where they slept. Thousands of people lived there, for more than a thousand years, and no house was greater than the rest. No palace. No king. [softly] Not yet.
- **opening_04_rivers** (bard): Then the farmers came down to the great rivers. Between the Tigris and the Euphrates there was little rain, but there was water, if you could lead it. They dug canals, and the mud gave back grain a hundredfold. At Eridu they built a shrine to the god of the sweet waters, and rebuilt it, layer upon layer, each one larger than the last. Copper came from the mountains. Someone had to share out the water, store the harvest, and judge between neighbours.
- **opening_05_uruk** (bard): Uruk grew until forty thousand people lived within its walls, the greatest city the world had seen. Its temples rose on platforms above the plain, and the priests who kept their storehouses pressed marks into clay to count the barley, the sheep, the beer and the workers. [pause] Counting became writing. And with writing came those who commanded what was written.
- **opening_06_polity** (bard): By five thousand years ago the towns had become something new. Kish and Ur, Lagash and Umma, each with its god, its ruler, its law, its fields and its border, and an army to hold them. On the Nile a king named Narmer wore the crowns of both Upper and Lower Egypt. The settlement had become a state: a power that could tax, build, and make war. [dramatically] Here, history begins.
- **opening_07_bridge** (bard): Three thousand years of kings followed. Bronze empires rose and fell together: Hittites and Egyptians, Mycenae and Ugarit, until the great collapse swept them away. Iron came, and alphabets, and coins. Assyria, Babylon and Persia each ruled the world they knew. Then Alexander of Macedon marched to the edge of India, and died at thirty-two, and his generals tore his empire apart. [pause] It is the year 300 before Christ. [dramatically] The world is yours to shape.
