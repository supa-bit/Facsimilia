# Kinds of government: a draft for you to check

This is for the Ledger question "The kinds of government" (Government,
laws and administration). You asked for a detailed list that grows with
history, about 25. Each line gives the kind, when it appears, real
examples, and what makes it play differently. Cross out, add or rename
anything, then answer the question on the Ledger.

## Monarchies

1. **Hellenistic kingship** (from 323 BC): the Ptolemies, Seleucids, Antigonids.
   The king's victories and gifts hold the army and the cities; legitimacy
   comes from victory and divine honours.
2. **Tribal kingship** (all eras): the Gauls, Thracians, Numidians.
   A king chosen from a royal kin by the warriors. Small, cheap to run, and
   splits easily.
3. **Divine pharaonic kingship** (until 30 BC): Ptolemaic Egypt.
   The temples back the throne, so Piety matters as much as Legitimacy.
4. **Principate** (27 BC to about AD 284): Augustus to the Severans.
   An emperor who rules behind the Republic's offices. The army and the
   Senate both have to be kept content.
5. **Dominate** (from AD 284): Diocletian, Constantine, the Byzantines.
   An open autocracy with a large court and civil service, and a stronger
   Authority.
6. **Feudal monarchy** (from about 900): France, England, Sicily.
   Land is held for service. The nobles are strong, and the royal lands
   are small.
7. **Absolute monarchy** (from about 1550): Louis XIV.
   A standing army and officials loyal to the crown; the estates are weak.
8. **Constitutional monarchy** (from 1689): Britain, later most kingdoms.
   Parliament holds the purse, and the king's choices are limited.

## Republics

9. **Aristocratic republic** (all eras): Rome, Carthage, Rhodes.
   Magistrates are elected from the leading families, and a Senate or
   council decides. Your family can stand for office.
10. **Democracy** (Athens and the Greek leagues).
    The assembly votes on war and laws: fast to anger, slow to plan.
11. **Federal league** (Aetolian and Achaean leagues, later the Swiss).
    Member cities keep their own laws; the league decides war.
12. **Merchant republic** (from about 1000): Venice, Genoa, the Dutch.
    Trade comes first and the leading families are merchants; strong at sea.
13. **Modern republic** (from 1776): France, the United States, Turkey.
    Parties, elections and a constitution.

## Religious rule

14. **Temple-state** (all eras): Comana, Olba, Pessinus.
    A high priest rules the temple's land.
15. **Caliphate** (from 632): the Rashidun, Umayyads, Abbasids.
    The successor to the Prophet leads the faithful, so Piety and
    Legitimacy are tied together.
16. **Papal state** (from 756).
    The Pope rules as a prince. Electors choose the next one, so there is
    no dynasty.
17. **Military-religious order** (from about 1100): the Hospitallers and
    the Teutonic Knights. Celibate knights rule, and members are recruited
    rather than born.

## Rule by soldiers and strongmen

18. **Tyranny** (Greek cities): Syracuse under Agathocles.
    One man holds the city by his guard. Strong at first, but the regime
    rarely outlives him.
19. **Military dictatorship** (from 1800): rule by the army after a coup.
20. **Mamluk sultanate** (1250 to 1517).
    Slave soldiers rule and choose the sultan from among themselves.

## Empires and their parts

21. **Satrapal empire** (Achaemenid style, early Seleucid).
    Great governors hold whole regions. Reach is cheap, but revolts are
    likely.
22. **Steppe khanate** (Scythians, Huns, Mongols).
    Mobile, ruled by kin and tribes, and split among heirs.
23. **Ottoman-style sultanate** (from 1300).
    A household of slave officials and land grants for service.

## Modern

24. **One-party state** (from 1917): the Soviet Union and others.
    The party holds every office; there are plans instead of markets.
25. **Parliamentary democracy** (from about 1900): coalitions, parties and
    a welfare state.

## Others to consider

These could be added:

- **Client kingdom**: a king who rules under another realm's protection
  (this may be better as a province kind).
- **Regency**: a temporary state of government while the ruler is a child
  (already a court question).
- **Personal union**: two crowns held by one ruler.
- **Colonial government**: a governor sent from abroad.
